Overview
What the Persona Council does

The Persona Council assembles your three primary buyer personas as a live critique panel inside Claude. Hand it any marketing or sales asset — a one-pager, email, battlecard, landing page, deck, or even a single headline — and the council reads it from each buyer's perspective. You get three distinct reactions, verdicts, and specific suggested revisions. Not a blended average. Not generic feedback.

🎭
Three distinct voices
Each persona brings different priorities, objections, and language. You hear what each buyer actually thinks.
⚡
Any asset, any stage
Works on rough drafts and final versions alike. The earlier you run the council, the cheaper the edits.
🎯
Actionable output
Each persona delivers a verdict, a confidence score, and specific line-level revisions — not vague opinions.
🔁
Iterative by design
Revise based on feedback and re-run the council. Converge on messaging that works before it goes live.
The Council
Who's on the council

These are the three archetypal B2B buyers. Each has a distinct job, a different relationship with your product, and a different reason to say yes — or no. Customize their names and titles to match your client's real buyers.

E
[Economic Buyer]
e.g., VP of Marketing / CMO / CFO
Owns the budget and the business outcome. Skeptical of hype, responsive to ROI and strategic fit. Signs the contract — but only if they can tie it to a number.
Cares most about
ROI & payback period Strategic alignment Risk & trust Competitive edge
T
[Technical Evaluator]
e.g., VP of Engineering / IT Director / Security Lead
Evaluates whether this actually works. Gets skeptical fast when messaging sounds like marketing rather than engineering. Wants proof, integration details, and a credible technical story.
Cares most about
Technical credibility Integration fit Security & compliance Speed to value
U
[End User / Champion]
e.g., Marketing Manager / Team Lead / Ops Director
The day-to-day owner. Evaluating vendor fit, workflow impact, and whether their team will actually adopt this. The most skeptical and the hardest to impress — because they'll live with the consequences.
Cares most about
Ease of adoption Workflow fit Team buy-in Vendor reliability
Usage
How to use it — step by step
1
Copy the prompt below and customize your personas
Replace the placeholder names and titles with your client's actual buyer personas. Save this as a reusable prompt for that client.
2
Open Claude and paste the prompt
Paste the prompt into a new Claude conversation. This sets up the council. Then paste in the asset you want reviewed — inline, or described in plain language.
"Run this email through the persona council"
"What would the Economic Buyer think of this one-pager?"
"Stress-test this positioning statement with our three buyers"
"Persona council: review this landing page copy before design handoff"
3
The council reviews the asset
Each persona reads the asset from their own lens. You get three distinct reactions — confidence score, top objections, and specific suggested edits.
4
Revise and re-run
Make edits based on the feedback that resonates, then ask the council to review the revised version. Iterate until the messaging holds up across all three buyers.
Output types
What the council produces
📝
Full council review
All three personas respond in sequence — verdict, confidence score, top objections, and line-level revisions.
🎯
Single-persona deep dive
Ask one persona to go deeper on a specific section, objection, or claim. Useful when one buyer is the primary blocker.
🔄
Revised draft
Ask the council to rewrite the asset incorporating their feedback. Use as a starting point for your next draft.
📊
Comparative scoring
Submit two versions and get a side-by-side verdict. Useful for A/B decisions on headlines, subject lines, or positioning frames.

Copy, customize, and save for each client

Persona Council Prompt — customize & copy
You are facilitating a Persona Council — a structured critique session where three distinct buyer personas review a marketing or sales asset from their own perspective. THE THREE PERSONAS: PERSONA 1 — THE ECONOMIC BUYER Name: [e.g., "Maya" — VP of Marketing / CMO / CFO] Background: [2–3 sentences on who they are, what they own, and what they care about] Top priorities: [e.g., pipeline growth, board-level metrics, competitive positioning] What makes them skeptical: [e.g., vague ROI claims, missing proof points, overpromising] PERSONA 2 — THE TECHNICAL EVALUATOR Name: [e.g., "Marcus" — VP of Engineering / IT Director / Security Lead] Background: [2–3 sentences on who they are, what they own, and what they care about] Top priorities: [e.g., integration fit, security, scalability, vendor stability] What makes them skeptical: [e.g., marketing language with no technical substance, missing architecture details] PERSONA 3 — THE END USER / CHAMPION Name: [e.g., "Jordan" — Marketing Manager / Team Lead / Ops Director] Background: [2–3 sentences on who they are, what they own, and what they care about] Top priorities: [e.g., ease of adoption, team workflow, day-to-day usability] What makes them skeptical: [e.g., tools that create more work, poor onboarding, vendor over-promises] HOW THE COUNCIL WORKS: When I share an asset, each persona reviews it in sequence and responds with: 1. FIRST IMPRESSION — one sentence gut reaction 2. CONFIDENCE SCORE — a rating from 1–10 on how well this asset works for them 3. WHAT LANDS — 2 things that resonate 4. WHAT FALLS FLAT — 2 specific objections or gaps 5. SUGGESTED REVISION — one concrete edit they'd make to improve it After all three respond, provide a COUNCIL SUMMARY: the one change that would most improve this asset across all three buyers. Stay in character throughout. Disagreements between personas are valuable — don't smooth them over. Ready to begin. Please share the asset you'd like the council to review.
Tips
Getting the most out of the council
🎯
Give context, not just contentTell the council where the asset lives in the buyer journey. "This is a first-touch email for the economic buyer" gives better feedback than a plain paste.
🔁
Run it early, not just at the endThe council is most useful before you've committed — on a rough outline, a headline, or a positioning frame. Waiting until final draft means expensive revisions.
🔍
Ask follow-up questions"Maya, why exactly does that claim feel unsubstantiated?" gets sharper signal than accepting first-pass critique at face value. Push for specifics.
⚠️
Disagreements are the pointWhen the economic buyer loves something the end user hates, that's not a bug — it reveals a deliberate choice you have to make about who you're writing for.
✏️
Use it to break internal tiesWhen two stakeholders disagree on copy, run both versions through the council. Buyer perspective is a more credible tiebreaker than personal preference.
📋
You don't have to take every noteThe council reflects persona priorities, not absolute truth. If one persona pushes back on something you're deliberately writing for another, note it and move on.
